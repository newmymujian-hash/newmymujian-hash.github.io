Where Are We in the U.S. Economic Cycle? A Spectral Decomposition

Decomposing U.S. industrial production and real GDP into Kitchin, Juglar, and Kuznets waves — and reading the current phase.

Jian Mu (Alex) · Nov 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Economic cycles are not one wave but several, superimposed. In Schumpeter’s nesting, one long Kondratieff wave contains roughly six Juglar cycles and eighteen Kitchin cycles; when the components peak together they amplify, and when they offset, they cancel. This note applies that framework to the longest U.S. samples available — industrial production (1919–2026) and real GDP (to 2026 Q3) — using Fourier decomposition with harmonic regression on trailing windows as the primary phase estimator, cross-checked with wavelet analysis.

The current phase (September 2026)

Component Fitted period Phase
Kitchin ~3.9 years Just past its peak
Juglar ~10 years Approaching its peak
Kuznets ~21 years Past its peak, declining
25–40 year band ~33 years Rising after its trough

A purely mechanical extrapolation of the composite cycle peaks around October 2026 and troughs around September 2028; across 81 window combinations, 96% place the cycle lower one year out. Read qualitatively, that points to a 2027 slowdown and a 2028 cyclical low — more like the 2015–16 industrial contraction than a full-blown recession. A Juglar–Kuznets resonant downturn window sits in the early 2030s.

The honest caveat

No frequency band’s spectral peak significantly beats an AR(1) red-noise benchmark at the 95% level, and the sample is too short to estimate a Kondratieff phase at all. Economic cycles are nonlinear, time-varying, and endogenous — not clockwork. Treat the phases above as a structured way of looking, not as a forecast with a date stamped on it.

This is general economic analysis for discussion, not investment advice.
